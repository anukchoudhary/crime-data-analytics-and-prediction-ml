Project Overview & Abstract

CrimeVision is an applied data science and machine learning project designed to study property-related crimes across urban sectors in India. By analyzing historical incident records covering Theft, Burglary, and Vehicle Theft across 7 Indian states from 2021 to 2025, the system identifies spatial hotspots, uncovers temporal trends, and generates zone-level risk estimations for specified dates and time windows.

Unlike generic dashboards that rely on static summaries or arbitrary scores, CrimeVision uses an integrated machine-learning pipeline: candidate classification models (XGBoost, Random Forest, Logistic Regression) are trained on preprocessed spatial and temporal features, and an empirical 5-factor weighted heuristic synthesizes historical incident density, diurnal window frequencies, and model probabilities into an actionable risk assessment.

System Architecture & Data Pipeline

1. Data Collection & Cleaning

Collection of 5,200 multi-state incident records across 36 urban sectors. Cleans timestamp inconsistencies, verifies latitude/longitude bounds, and removes corrupted records.

2. Feature Engineering

Extraction of 8 standardized 3-hour diurnal windows (e.g. 9 PM – 12 AM), calendar seasons (Winter, Summer, Monsoon, Autumn), and centroid spatial aggregation.

3. Model Training & Validation

Preprocessed with StandardScaler (numerical) and OneHotEncoder (categorical). Evaluated with 80/20 train-test split across Accuracy, Precision, Recall, and Confusion Matrix.

4. Fast Production Inference

Optimized zero-dependency vectorized tree evaluation in pure Python/NumPy, enabling sub-millisecond predictions within Vercel serverless function memory limits.

Academic Privacy & Ethical Safeguards

CrimeVision operates exclusively at the aggregate municipal zone and property-crime category level. In compliance with data ethics guidelines, the system contains no suspect profiling, no individual scoring, no facial recognition, and no collection of personal identifying information (PII) like names, addresses, or phone numbers. All predictions are statistical estimates derived from historical patterns.
